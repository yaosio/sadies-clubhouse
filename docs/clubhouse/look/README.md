# Art style: the misremembered 90s

The look every part of Sadie's Clubhouse shares. Read before changing how anything looks. Each
activity's own look is in its own docs folder.

- `clubhouse.md`: the clubhouse itself (the house, the hall, the grounds, the controls on screen):
  what the owner has approved.
- `history.md`: the menu and test room that came before the clubhouse, and their mock-ups. Only for
  reference.
- How it's drawn in code: `docs/clubhouse/world/drawing.md`.

## The idea
Everything here is lost software: 90s shareware that was never finished but always promised a full
version soon. It looks like the 90s as people *remember* them, not as they were. Anyone seeing it
should think "yeah, that's from the 90s", even though it does things no 90s PC could. Early DOS,
mid-90s multimedia and late-90s shine are mashed together into programs that never existed.

- **Colors:** bright and loud, like Kid Pix. Never drab.
- **True to the 90s:** chunky low-res pixels, a small palette, ordered dithering (checkerboard and
  crosshatch dots) instead of smooth gradients, tiny blocky text.
- **Impossible for the 90s ("strangely well made"):** smooth squishy physics with many pieces,
  shading that's too careful and soft for the era, expressive characters, glossy highlights,
  sparkles and lens flare, crisp on any screen.
- **An activity on a computer is a DOS program that invented its own look** (no gray Windows look,
  no title bars or close boxes), in the candy-purple frame of the first activity on a computer
  (`docs/dropper-world/look.md`). The second has its own copy of that look: moving it into the
  toolbox would mean retesting every activity, so it waits until the frame next changes in both.
- **One that lives in its room or building** is part of the 3D world instead, with no frame.
- **The never-finished feeling** comes from the promises: "CHAPTER 2 COMING SOON 1996!", locked
  tools, save only in the full version. **No prices and no order buttons** (the owner said no).
