HOW THE DASHBOARD WORKS

  • It always shows someone. Sadie to start with, since she's the star.
  • Phone: one slim strip under the board with their face, name, mood, what they're doing, and their strongest feeling as one LED meter. Tap it (or the ▲ key) and a small window pops up over the bottom of the board saying why they're doing it. Tap again to put it away.
  • Phone: the LED sign only appears when there's news. It covers the strip for a few seconds, then gets out of the way.
  • Phone: the character stamps sit in the key bar, next to F1 HELP and TOYS, so they don't need a row of their own. F3 SOUND is left off the phone to make room.
  • Wide screen: everything goes in a column down the right side. Wide screens have width to spare, and the tower grows up, so the board keeps its full height.
  • Tap a character on the board or their stamp to switch. Chooter's stamp shows up once he's moved in; the padlocked empty ones (wide screen only) are the "more friends coming" promise.
  • Only the strongest feeling shows, not all of them. When a different one takes over (Sadie gets hungrier than she is impatient), the meter switches to it. It's the thing driving what they do, so it's the one worth seeing.
  • A nice side effect: the camera no longer has to lean up to make room for a bubble, so it moves by itself less.

EACH ACTIVITY GETS ITS OWN

The frame belongs to the Play Place and looks the same in every activity: the top strip with ESC BACK and the activity's logo, and the key bar with F1 HELP. The middle is each activity's own. Dropper World fills it with the stamps and this dashboard. A future activity could put a paint palette there, or a scoreboard, or nothing at all.

On a phone the board gets almost the whole screen: only the top strip, the slim dashboard strip and the key bar take space, about a sixth of it (the first version took over a third).
